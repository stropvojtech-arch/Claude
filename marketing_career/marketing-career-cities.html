The factors behind each score

3-year average = (junior + mid + halfway between mid and senior) ÷ 3, after paying new-build rent. Savings = that disposable income minus your subscriptions (and padel, if counted), over 36 months. Groceries are already inside disposable income; their factor adds a quality score.

New-build rent (1-bedroom, under ~20 years old)

Stockholm and Gothenburg new-build first-hand flats go through housing queues, so a newcomer usually rents second-hand at a higher price. Stockholm uses a second-hand estimate.

Grocery quality (judgement, 1–10)

How each factor is scored
  • Disposable income (3-year average): 0 kr = 0, 15,000 kr or more = 10.
  • Rent share: new-build rent ÷ 3-year average net salary. 25% or less = 10, 55% or more = 0.
  • Global subscriptions: 2× Claude Pro, Google AI Pro, Netflix Standard and Higgsfield (about US$29) as a share of disposable income. 0% = 10, 20% or more = 0.
  • Groceries: 40% affordability (grocery bill ÷ disposable; 0% = 10, 40% = 0) + 60% quality score.
  • Padel (only when “Count padel” is on): your share of a court hour × sessions per month, as a share of disposable. 0% = 10, 15% or more = 0.
  • Everything else: the going-out score from the section below (coffee, meals, gym, karting).
  • Weights come from your ranking using rank-order centroid weights. Without padel, the first priority gets about 46% and the last 4%. With padel, 41% and 3%.

Global subscriptions · new variable

Local spending versus global spending

Some of what you buy is priced locally (coffee, meals, sport). Some is priced globally: Claude, Google and Netflix cost roughly the same everywhere. A city with cheap local prices but lower pay may let you go out more while making global subscriptions a bigger share of your money. This section shows both measures side by side.

Monthly subscriptions as a share of disposable income

Prices include VAT or GST. Each is divided by the city's mid-level disposable income. Small figure underneath = price in DKK.

Top three by money left

Purchasing power map

Copenhagen = 100 on both axes. Further right means your money buys more going out. Higher means it buys more global subscriptions. Points off the diagonal mean one kind of spending goes further than the other.

How the measures are built

  • Local purchasing power = disposable income ÷ the city's going-out basket, indexed to Copenhagen.
  • Global purchasing power = disposable income ÷ the city's subscription bundle, indexed to Copenhagen.
  • Profile: if one index is more than 10% above the other, the city leans that way. Otherwise it is balanced.
  • Left to save = disposable income − going-out basket − subscriptions.

Sources (Oct 2026): Netflix Standard prices from bigmacindex.com, geopriced.com and opentherank.com trackers; Claude Pro from geopriced.com and Anthropic's net EU price of €18 plus local VAT; Google AI Pro from opentherank.com and Google's US price. Netflix, Claude and Google are not sold in mainland China, so Shanghai uses US prices (1 USD = 6.40 DKK) plus a VPN estimate.

Career opportunities · 45% of the score

Where the marketing jobs are

Two steps. First, how deep is the marketing job market: twelve sub-scores from 1 to 10, averaged. Second, what share of those jobs can you actually do in English only. The career pillar is the first number times the second.

How deep the market is (all 10 cities). The share is an estimate for marketing jobs in that city. The hard data is national and covers all jobs: in 2021, 11.7% of online job ads in Denmark were written in English, 12.4% in Sweden, 12.2% in the Netherlands, and 7.3% in Austria and Czechia (OECD Working Paper 294, Table 3.2). Marketing and professional jobs in capital cities run well above those averages, so the shares here are set higher. Treat them as judgement, not measurement.

PRG  Prague finances · 1 CZK = 0.306 DKK

Prague is cheap in kroner but not cheap relative to Czech pay. Rent takes 42% of a mid-level net salary, about the same as Copenhagen (41%). Local prices are lower than in Copenhagen, but they take a bigger share of what's left: a month of going out takes 29% of disposable income, against 19% in Copenhagen. A junior ends the month 158 kr short.

Salary: Glassdoor Prague, 2026 (marketing specialist median ≈47k Kč total pay, marketing manager ≈83k, head of marketing ≈98k). Tax: 2026 rules, 15% on gross up to 1,762,812 Kč a year, 2,570 Kč monthly taxpayer credit, 7.1% social and 4.5% health insurance. Rent: Numbeo and expat guides (Oct 2026). Lítačka 30-day pass 550 Kč. Weekday lunch menu average 226 Kč (Edenred, April 2026).

DXB  Dubai finances · no income tax, 1 AED = 1.74 DKK

Dubai is the only city where gross pay is take-home pay: no income tax and no social contributions for foreigners. Rent is the catch. Leases are usually paid upfront in 1–4 cheques, and a junior on AED 8,000 ends the month 3,174 kr short after a median 1-bedroom. From mid level the money flips: a senior keeps 43,806 kr, the most of any city here. Lifestyle scores lowest of the ten (6.2): five months of 40 °C+ heat, poor walkability and almost no cycling.

Salary: GulfTalent Dubai (marketing executive average AED 5,500, up to 10,000), Payscale 2026 (marketing manager ≈AED 12,150), and 2025–26 postings (marketing manager AED 15,000–25,000, marketing director AED 30,000–40,000). Figures used assume a European-educated hire: AED 8,000 / 18,000 / 35,000. Rent: Binayah and Kensington 2026 listings (JVC ≈AED 80,000 a year, Business Bay 85,000–110,000, Marina 95,000–120,000); median AED 6,700 a month. Utilities include DEWA plus the 5% municipality housing fee. Health insurance is employer-paid by law. Netflix AED 49 (Netflix UAE), Google AI Pro AED 76.99. Padel: AED 200–250 an hour at standard courts (Bayut, player reports May 2026). Coffee, meals, gym and karting are estimates.

Methodology

How the score is built

October 2026 additions: figures come from Glassdoor, Payscale, Seek, StepStone, Robert Half, local salary boards, Otodom, ImmoScout24, Rightmove/ONS, Citylets, StreetEasy/Zumper, liv.rent, Domain, Madlan/WeCheck and Numbeo, with 2026 tax rules computed per country (Scottish bands for Edinburgh; German tax class I with social contributions; Israeli tax with Bituach Leumi, health tax and the mandatory 6% pension). Many salaries, Swiss, Australian and German rents, and most café, gym, padel and karting prices are estimates (marked est). German rents are warm rents (heating included). Austin transport is a car (US$650 a month), not a bus pass. UK utilities include council tax; Tel Aviv utilities include arnona. Polish junior tax applies the under-26 income-tax exemption. Tel Aviv uses high-tech pay, the realistic route without Hebrew. FX: CAD 4.64, USD 6.40, GBP 8.55, CHF 7.95, PLN 1.76, ILS 1.78, NZD 3.636, ARS 0.00417 (1,535 per USD), CRC 0.0133.

Disposable income = net salary − median rent − essential costs. Health insurance is only listed where it is a separate mandatory cost (Amsterdam). Singapore assumes no CPF contributions for foreigners. Dubai has no income tax, and employers must pay health insurance. Vienna's 13th and 14th salaries are spread over twelve months.
